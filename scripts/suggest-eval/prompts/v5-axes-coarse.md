Conference tracks in a scientific field usually mix two kinds of category, and a good set has both:
- approach categories: the kind of work (for example computational methods, statistical analysis, experimental/functional work, machine learning, clinical translation, population-level studies, ethics and policy);
- application categories: a domain studied, but only domains that are fields in their own right at this meeting, with their own community and many presentations.

Tracks are coarse. Each is a broad area a researcher would recognise from conference programs in this field. Don't make a category for a single technology, assay, molecular layer, organ system or disease unless a large share of the program is about it; fold such work into the broad approach category it belongs to.

Read every presentation (title and abstract). Then define 10–13 categories in the field's conventional vocabulary, each holding at least 4% of presentations and none more than about a third.

Assign each presentation to every category it substantively belongs to: one approach category when one clearly applies, plus an application category when the work targets one. Most presentations get one or two categories, some three.
