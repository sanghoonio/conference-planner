Conference tracks in a scientific field usually mix two kinds of category, and a good set has both:
- approach categories: the kind of work (computational methods, statistical analysis, experimental/functional work, machine learning, clinical translation, population/epidemiological studies, ethics and policy…);
- application categories: the domain studied (disease areas, organ systems, life stages, populations…).

Read every presentation (title and abstract). Then define 8–15 categories in the field's conventional vocabulary, including both approach and application categories, each broad enough to hold at least 3% of presentations and none holding more than about a third.

Assign each presentation to every category it substantively belongs to: one approach category when one clearly applies, plus one application category when the work targets a specific domain. Most presentations end up with one or two categories, some with three.
