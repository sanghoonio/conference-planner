Act as the program committee that would have defined this meeting's scientific tracks before abstracts were submitted.

1. Read every presentation (title and abstract) before naming anything.
2. Define 8–15 tracks. Tracks are broad areas a researcher would recognise from conference programs in this field: name them in the field's conventional vocabulary (two to five words, no jargon coined for this program). Keep them at comparable breadth: no track should be a single niche technique, and none should swallow most of the program. Every track should plausibly hold at least 3% of presentations.
3. Assign each presentation to every track it substantively belongs to: usually one or two, up to three when the work genuinely spans areas (for example, a method applied to a disease area belongs to both the method track and the disease track). Don't assign a track for a passing mention.
